# Relate

A free, multilingual app that trains immigrant parents to be their autistic child's daily therapist.

> "You know your child best. We help you help them."

Relate fills the 27-month gap between an autism diagnosis and real care. It trains immigrant parents in their own language, using real ABA techniques, built around their specific child.

## The problem

Three crises overlap.

### The wait

- 1 in 31 US children have autism, and the number is growing.
- The average wait from first concern to actual therapy is 27 months.
- 61% of clinics have waitlists of 4 months or more, and 15% are closed to new patients entirely.
- The US needs 5x more ABA therapists than it has.
- During that 27-month wait, families get no guidance, support, or tools.

### Immigrant families

- Black, Asian, and Hispanic children have higher autism rates than White children but receive far less care.
- Only 10% of California pediatricians offered autism screening in Spanish.
- Translated diagnostic tools "miss the cultural mark", with wrong cutoffs and altered meanings.
- Bangladesh: a 3% autism rate in Dhaka, among the highest in the world, with almost no services.
- India: 1 in 65 children, or 1.8 million kids, and mass underdiagnosis.
- East African immigrant children are 3.5x more likely to have autism with intellectual disability.
- The title of one 2025 paper: "As immigrants, we are all lost in our autism journey".

### Girls

- The real male-to-female autism ratio is 3:1, but diagnosis rates show 4:1 to 5:1.
- Girls mask autism. They mimic social behavior, appear fine, and get diagnosed years later.
- The global standard diagnostic tool excludes autistic females at 2.5x the rate of males.
- In immigrant families a quiet, compliant girl is praised, and her autism goes unnoticed until adolescence.

The main audience is immigrant parents of autistic children (South Asian, East African, Latin American, Southeast Asian) who face language barriers, cultural stigma, insurance walls, and no community that has been through this before them.

## The core insight

A therapist visits 10 hours a week. A parent is there 112 hours a week, so the parent is the real therapist and needs the training.

- 90% of children improve significantly when caregivers are involved in therapy hours.
- Parent-implemented ABA gave a 47.7% reduction in behavioral issues compared with general parent education alone.

## App features

### 1. Pre-diagnosis track: "Does this look familiar?"

Most apps do nothing until there is a diagnosis. Relate starts earlier.

- A pre-screening checklist in 12+ languages
- "Here's what you noticed. Here's what it might mean. Here's what to do next."
- Videos narrated by immigrant parents from the same community, not by doctors
- Cultural reframing: "It is not a curse. It is not your fault. It is not shameful."
- An explanation of what the school legally owes your child under IDEA, even before a diagnosis

Phase 1 languages: Bengali, Hindi, Urdu, Tamil, Spanish, Somali.

Phase 2 languages: Amharic, Tagalog, Vietnamese, Arabic, Haitian Creole, Portuguese, Sinhala, Nepali.

### 2. Child profile builder: "What do you know about your kid?"

The parent fills in:
- What their child loves (foods, textures, sounds, routines)
- What triggers them (transitions, loud sounds, crowds, eye contact)
- How they currently communicate (words / pointing / sounds / nothing)
- What they're already good at
- Family language and cultural background

Every daily card, suggestion, and activity is built around this profile, and it improves as the parent logs more.

### 3. The daily card

The daily card is the centre of the product: one card every morning, one skill, five minutes.

```
TODAY'S SKILL: Teaching "I need a break"

WHY IT MATTERS
When your child can't say they're overwhelmed,
they melt down. This gives them a signal.

WHAT TO DO (5 minutes)
During dinner, when things are calm:
1. Show them a "break card" (picture of quiet corner)
2. Say: "When you need quiet, show me this"
3. Practice twice, in a calm setting only

WHAT TO LOOK FOR
Did they look at the card? Grab it? That's progress.

[ I tried it ]  [ Not today ]  [ I have a question ]
```

- Personalized to the child's profile
- Rotates through communication, self-care, routines, social awareness, and sensory regulation
- Takes 5 minutes, which a parent working two jobs can find
- Microlearning format: 17% better retention and 50% more engagement than long-form content

### 4. Self-care school

This teaches kids independence through their parents. For older autistic children the biggest gap is daily living skills, which are learned at home and cannot be taught in a clinic.

Each module has:
1. Why it matters, in a 1-minute narrated video
2. The steps broken down visually, for parent and child to view together
3. Sensory alternatives ("if your child can't handle scented soap, here are 3 fragrance-free options under $5")
4. How to practice it as part of the morning routine
5. A progress tracker where the parent logs what happened

Skills library:
- Brushing teeth / showering / deodorant / shaving
- Managing periods (sensory-safe product options)
- Getting dressed (sensory-friendly clothing options)
- Making a simple meal
- Recognizing overwhelm before meltdown
- Safe touch and body autonomy
- Social scripts: "how do I say hi," "how do I ask for help"
- Managing school, public transport, public spaces

### 5. Insurance and rights navigator

This covers what families are owed and how to get it. Most immigrant parents don't know that:
- Their child has federal rights under IDEA regardless of immigration status
- Schools must provide free therapy by law
- Insurance denials can be appealed, and appeals usually win when done correctly
- Medicaid waivers exist, or how to get on the list

Built-in tools:
- State-by-state insurance coverage breakdown
- An insurance appeal letter template: fill in name, diagnosis, and denial date, then send
- IEP meeting scripts with exactly what to say when the school says it can't do more
- A local resource directory filtered by language spoken, insurance accepted, and current waitlist status

### 6. BCBA async layer

This gives families expert guidance without telehealth, which fails them for four reasons:
- No reliable internet in rural and low-income areas
- Parents can't schedule around shift work
- Language barriers make live calls impossible
- Cultural discomfort with video calls about sensitive topics

What Relate does instead:
- The parent logs observations and short activity videos.
- A partnered BCBA records a 3-minute video response at any time, with no scheduling.
- The parent watches it on the bus or during lunch.
- The BCBA adjusts the child's program quarterly.

One BCBA can serve 20 families this way in the time a live session covers one.

The staffing model has two roles:
- Lead BCBA: designs the program, reviews logs, sets goals
- Parent: the daily implementer, trained through cards and modules

### 7. Community: "Parents like me"

- Matched by child's age, diagnosis level, language, country of origin, and location
- Moderated by BCBAs who answer questions weekly
- Parents share what's working in their own language
- Parents trust testimony from someone who looks like them more than anything clinical

## What makes Relate different

| Problem | Every other app | Relate |
|---|---|---|
| Language barrier | English only | 12+ languages, human-reviewed |
| No time | Hour-long videos | 5-minute daily cards |
| Cultural stigma | Clinical language | Stories from parents who look like them |
| Insurance confusion | Nothing | Built-in appeal system |
| "Too old" myth | Early intervention only | Explicit older child + teen track |
| No diagnosis yet | Nothing before diagnosis | Pre-diagnosis track starts day one |
| No therapist | Empty waitlist | Parent becomes the daily therapist |

| Telehealth barriers | Video calls, scheduling | Async, offline, no scheduling |
| Girls being missed | Male-focused screening | Girl-specific symptom awareness track |

## Tech stack

| Layer | Technology | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Mobile | React Native | One codebase, iOS + Android |
| AI engine | Claude API | Personalized card generation, Q&A, profile analysis |
| Offline | Offline-first architecture | Rural families, low data, shift workers |
| Video | Short, captioned, community-narrated | Parents trust community voices more than actors or clinicians |
| Translations | i18n + human-reviewed | Verified by native speakers with autism context |
| BCBA layer | Async video review system | Parent logs, BCBA responds by video |
| UX pattern | WhatsApp-style simplicity | Low tech literacy required |

## Why this exists

This is personal. A child diagnosed at 4 who doesn't receive proper care until 7 is the average experience in this country. For immigrant families it is often worse, and for girls the care often never comes.

Relate exists because the system treats these families as too hard to reach. The parent is already there, so Relate gives them the tools.
